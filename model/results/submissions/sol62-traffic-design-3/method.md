The network computes color-opponent, luminance, and local luminance contrast channels, with spatial normalization and clipping. A dense layer correlates those features with procedural sign templates built from polygons, circles, and hand-written strokes. Max pooling selects the best match for each class across translations, scales, aspect ratios, and rotations. All weights are generated from formulas and general sign geometry, without fitting or copying training images.

Two labeled smoke checks used the same 341 training images. The first revealed frequent confusion between red-bordered signs and stop or no-vehicles signs. The second showed that adding a separate local-contrast channel improved accuracy from 20.82% to 26.69% on that subset. The held-out accuracy is unknown.
