I used a fully data-independent matched-shape network. Its front end normalizes brightness and contrast, applies analytic Gaussian-derivative filters in eight orientations (plus Laplacian and smoothing responses), rectifies them, combines opposite polarities, and pools to a 14 x 14 oriented-contour representation.

The final convolution contains schematic ASL hand models that I drew in `build.py` from general knowledge: palms and wrists are ellipses/capsules, fingers are articulated capsules, and flexion lines/inter-finger clefts supply internal detail. Each class has equal numbers of deterministic variants spanning small rotation, scale, drawing style, and light/dark hand polarity. The weights are normalized synthetic feature maps, not training examples or statistics. A 5 x 5 correlation map and max pooling provide translation and nuisance tolerance, and the resulting 24 maxima are the class scores. No optimization, fitting, image-derived coordinates, or image-derived weights were used.
