# Method

**Architecture.** I hand-built a feature circuit followed by a hand-written naive-Bayes readout (1.35M weights, about 122k nonzero).
- **Colour branch.** Three 1x1 conv+relu layers turn each pixel's RGB into soft colour categories using colour-cone thresholds I picked by hand: sky/water blue, light (sky) blue, white, vegetation green, warm/brown, vivid red paint, vivid yellow paint, dark, neutral grey. Saturation comes out as half the sum of |R-G|, |G-B| and |B-R|. Clamps are built as differences of two relus, and AND gates as relu(sum - (n-1)). Dense layers then take the fraction of each category in fixed regions: top band, bottom band, outer frame, centre.
- **Texture and structure.** I use fixed formula filters only. Sobel gradient energy is thresholded into a per-pixel "smooth" map (sky, walls, blurred backgrounds). On contrast-normalised luminance (the `norm` op) I compute central differences and the same differences smeared over 7 px along the edge. For pure noise the smeared/plain ratio is 1/sqrt(7); for a long straight edge it is 1. This "straightness" ratio and the horizontal/vertical edge balance are read through ratio steps (relu(num - a*den) ramps). A horizon cue is the largest full-width row-mean luminance step, taken with reshape and maxpool.
- **Cells and object mask.** On 4x4-pixel average cells I measure how uniform the frame is, and the colour difference between centre and frame and between top and bottom. Pixels whose colour is far from the frame's mean colour form an object mask. From it I take the object's size and its second moments along x and along y, giving an elongation ratio.
- **Readout.** Each of the 25 cues passes through hand-placed knots, so the class score is piecewise linear in the cue. Class k adds w_cue * log P(cue bin | k), taken from belief tables I typed from general knowledge of the ten classes. Examples: planes have sky above and below; ships have darker water below; vehicles have vivid paint, straight edges and dark ground under them; frogs have close-up texture with no sky; deer have green, textured surroundings around a brown centre.

**How the weights were produced.** Every number is written in `build.py`: colour thresholds, filter formulas, region masks, knots and belief tables. Nothing is read from files, and nothing is fitted, regressed, averaged or clustered from the training images. I looked at grids of training images (saved as PNGs) only for a qualitative feel for the classes. Before spending checks I unit-tested two things in plain Python, without the network and without images: the colour formulas on hand-typed reference colours (e.g. "sky blue (0.5, 0.65, 0.85)"), and the belief tables on hand-typed "typical scene" cue vectors. The second test found inconsistencies, for example a cat row that put so much mass on "no green / no blue" that cat won almost every scene.

**Checks used (4 of 5).** Each was on a different random 5,000-image training subset.
1. 27.2%: the straightness steps were mis-placed. Sobel smoothing makes even noise look straight, so vehicles absorbed animals. Fix: unsmoothed differences and smooth-pixel fractions.
2. 31.4%: ship and cat were over-predicted, and bird and dog were never chosen. Fix: ship must rely on water/sky evidence; dog and bird got distinct signatures; correlated "absence" cues were down-weighted.
3. 31.7%: planes still went to ship. Fix: split bottom-of-frame blue into light (sky) and dark (water), and add white-bottom and dark-ground cues.
4. 32.4%: this is the submitted network.

Each revision changed my stated beliefs for a reason I could explain. I did not tune biases to balance predictions and did not keep the best of many variants. The weakest classes remain bird and horse, and the cat/dog and car/truck pairs.
