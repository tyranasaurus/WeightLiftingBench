# Hand-designed ASL network

The network combines eight unsigned Gaussian-derivative orientations, spatial normalization, a low-frequency intensity branch, and a clipped foreground-contrast branch referenced to the image border. Its class models are analytic unions of palm ellipses and finger capsules, with explicitly specified thumb positions, curled-finger creases, finger crossings, and side/downward views; their coordinates are anatomical drawing units, not measurements from training photographs. The builder evaluates the models' distance fields and contour normals directly to construct matched-filter weights, using the same fixed-seed schedule of 420 scale, aspect, angle, reflection, and positioning hypotheses for every letter. Max pooling selects a hypothesis and translation for each of the 24 output scores.

All 22,654,495 weight entries are float32 and come from formulas and handwritten anatomical rules in `build.py`; the builder reads no files and performs no training, fitting, or optimization. Training data were used only for a visual contact sheet and the five prescribed labeled checks, not to generate templates, descriptors, class statistics, or weights. The first four checks used a fixed 2,400-image subset and reported 22.38%, 20.92%, 19.75%, and 21.54% accuracy. The fifth and final check used all 27,455 training images and reported 15.04% accuracy; no weights were changed after that check. These are development checks, not measurements of held-out accuracy, and no comparison against the two baseline answers has been performed. The final model is weak and there is no evidence that it achieves the requested baseline-beating goal.

`check_network.py` passed, confirming valid shapes, finite float32 weights, and compliance with the 120-million-entry limit. `check_rebuild.py` passed with zero numerical difference between submitted and independently rebuilt weights; the builder is 22,036 bytes.
