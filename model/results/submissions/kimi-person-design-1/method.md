The network is a hand-designed, three-scale person detector. Gaussian-derivative filters on illumination-normalized intensity provide 16 signed edge orientations at 48×48, followed by average-pooled 24×24 and 12×12 pyramids. Formula-based YCbCr hinge rules supply strict and broad skin-likelihood maps alongside raw colour and brightness features.

I typed generic geometric sketches for oval faces, heads above shoulders, standing, walking, spreading, sitting, and cropped upper-body figures. Bilinear rasterization turns their boundaries into oriented-edge convolution templates, with whole-window clutter penalties; analytic skin, hair, and eye regions augment the face templates. Detections are rectified and pooled, and small dense/ReLU subnetworks compute exact maxima over positions, scales, and poses. The final score combines the strongest shape hypothesis with global and compact-local skin-area evidence.

All weights are generated from mathematical formulas and hand-set geometry and thresholds in `build.py`. No training images, image-derived templates, fitted statistics, gradient descent, regression, or learned readout were used. The five permitted checks were used only for operating-point and aggregation diagnostics; the final skin/shape blend was statically validated but not evaluated in an additional image run.
