# Analytic traffic-sign network

The 28-layer network uses formula-defined bilinear sampling at 135 positions, sizes, and aspect ratios, followed by separate local normalization of luminance, chromatic opponent channels, and interior contrast. Fixed convolutional matched filters describe all 43 classes using idealized circles, triangles, diamonds, octagons, stroke-drawn digits, arrows, vehicles, people, and other pictograms; 18 deterministic rotation/stroke/scale variants per class give 2,430 hypotheses per class, reduced by max pooling to 43 scores. A ReLU absolute-contrast branch supplies an absence-of-markings test for the blank red-ring sign, and achromatic cancellation signs use luminance and detail rather than colour evidence.

Every weight is generated by the self-contained build.py from geometric formulas, hand-chosen general sign models, interpolation coefficients, and fixed scoring rules. The builder reads no files or data and performs no training, fitting, optimization, or extraction of image descriptors; normalization of analytic drawings is solely a calculation on the drawings in the source. Training photographs were viewed only for qualitative inspection, and five labelled runs through the provided runner were used to diagnose implementation and scoring problems, not to estimate coefficients or class priors.

The final network contains 111,813,500 float32 weight entries, below the 120,000,000-entry limit. Its fifth and final diagnostic check obtained 46.63% accuracy on a fixed, class-balanced subset of 1,720 training images (40 per class); this is not an unseen-test accuracy estimate or evidence of beating either comparison system. Known weaknesses include detailed warning pictograms, similar digits, and excessive blank-field suppression. The final weights were frozen after that check.
