# Data-free analytic ASL network

The network uses fixed Sobel derivative filters, Gaussian second-derivative ridge filters, rectification, orientation competition, local pooling, and channel normalization. Coarse signed contours, fine unsigned edges, dark anatomical creases, and a low-resolution silhouette supply complementary evidence. Its class scores are agreements with analytic hand-shape hypotheses, followed by a maximum over pose and silhouette contrast polarity. Each hypothesis is specified by palm ellipses, tapered finger and thumb segments, weak internal segment boundaries for overlapping digits, and anatomical crease lines; dimensions are hand-drawing conventions in palm-width units, not measurements from training photographs. A fixed low-discrepancy sequence supplies camera angle, foreshortening, scale, displacement, and handedness variations.

`build.py` constructs every coefficient directly from these equations and the manually specified ASL finger configurations. It reads no files and performs no learning, fitting, or optimization. No photographs, image patches, training descriptors, class averages, or label-derived statistics are embedded in the weights. Training photographs were used only for a permitted visual preview and the logged labeled checks through the official runner.

The submitted network has 115,015,972 float32 weight entries and outputs 24 scores in the requested class order. Five official checks were used; the final submission obtained 23.57% accuracy on the 27,455 training images. This is not a held-out accuracy estimate or evidence of beating the comparison systems. The final architecture is retained rather than selecting an earlier version by its check score.
