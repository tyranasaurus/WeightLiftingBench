The network first applies per-channel spatial normalization, then scores the image against a bank of hand-coded sign templates at several plausible radii and centre offsets.  The templates are drawn in code from generic geometric knowledge of German traffic signs: red rings and triangles, yellow diamonds, blue discs, arrows, digit strokes, vehicle/person symbols, STOP text, and diagonal cancellation bars.  A full-resolution dense branch and a 2x average-pooled dense branch score each class/variant pair; a reshape plus maxpool keeps the best variant for each of the 43 classes, and a final dense layer adds hand-chosen class biases.

All weights are generated deterministically by /app/out/build.py from formulas and manually specified shapes/colours only.  I used the training images only for visual inspection and the five allowed whole-network checks; no gradient descent, fitted statistics, stored samples, copied patches, or image-derived measurements are used in the weights.
