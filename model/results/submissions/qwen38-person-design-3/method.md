# Method: hand-designed person detector for 96x96 COCO photos

## Architecture
All weights are generated by `build.py` from formulas and typed-in shape models of
people; no data is read and nothing is fitted.

1. **Base maps (96x96).** Gaussian-smoothed luminance/RGB (`sm`); Sobel gradient
   magnitude `e` (|gx|+|gy|, steepened and clipped); a soft **skin mask** `sk`
   (relu of 1 minus 5x the sum of eight hand-chosen RGB rule violations:
   R>G>B ordering, minimum brightness per channel, limits on over-red/over-orange);
   a soft **dark mask** `dk` (L < 0.38) for hair/eyes/dark cloth.
2. **Per-image contrast gain control.** Each of `e`, `sk`, `dk` is combined
   (soft-AND via `relu(a+b-t)`) with a saliency map built from the `norm` op
   (per-image mean/variance). This makes a uniform skin-coloured wooden wall or a
   uniformly dark room score ~0: cues measure *standing out in this image*.
3. **Pyramid.** `e` by maxpool, `sk`/`dk` by avgpool, x2 three times (48/24/12).
4. **Parametric correlation templates** over [e, sk, dk] at each level, three
   families, several radii per level (heads from ~3 px to ~50 px):
   * `face`: skin disc + dark eye pair at +-0.4r + dark hair cap + mouth hint,
     edge ring, skin annulus suppression, and negative dk lobes where a face is
     *not* dark (cheeks/nose bridge, and the animal muzzle point below the mouth),
     plus a far-below skin suppression lobe (tree trunks).
   * `hs` (head-shoulders): edge ring on the head, horizontal shoulder band at
     +1.3 head radii spanning +-2.2r, vertical torso/arm side bands at +-1.9r,
     skin face disc, dark hair cap.
   * `sk` (skin blob): compact skin disc with surround suppression, internal edge
     texture and a dark-above lobe (profile faces, hands, limbs).
   Every kernel is zero-DC per channel (uniform fields respond 0) and normalised
   by its raw positive mass, so a perfect match scores ~1. Linearity of conv lets
   the three per-channel convs be summed with `add` before one relu.
5. **Decision.** Per template: global max -> threshold + gain + clip -> evidence
   d in [0,1]; per family: max over scales/levels (built from dense/relu/add as
   scalar max chains). Final rule table:
   `p = 1.00*D_face + 0.90*D_hs + 0.40*D_sk`, person iff `p > 0.85`,
   emitted as scores `(0.85-p, p-0.85)` by three 1->2 dense layers plus `add`.
   One strong cue or two medium cues fire; a lone skin blob never does.

Total: 340-ish layers, ~309k weights (limit 120M).

## How the weights were produced
Every number comes from code in `build.py`: Gaussian/derivative/ring/sector
formulas for filters, anatomical proportions for the shape models (eye spacing
~0.8 head radii, shoulder line ~1.3 head radii below head centre, shoulders
~2.2 head radii half-width), hand-picked RGB skin rules and thresholds.
`python3 /app/check_rebuild.py /app/out` confirms build.py reproduces the
submitted weights with no data present.

## Checks and what they taught (5/5 used, logged in checks.log)
1. v1 thresholds too high: everything predicted "no person".
2. Thresholds lowered without gain control: fires everywhere (57% FP) - raw
   correlation matches any clutter blob.
3. Added norm-based saliency gating: clutter stopped firing, but so did people.
4. Probed a lower final threshold: still ~nothing fires -> not a calibration
   issue. Analysis found the template normalisation divided by zero-DC positive
   mass that real images can never activate, capping responses ~0.45.
5. Fixed normalisation + steeper edge gain: a few hundred fires; responses still
   below threshold on most real people.

With the check budget spent, further debugging used **synthetic unit tests only**
(formula-drawn cartoons: frontal/back/grey/small person, wood wall, checker
clutter, horse, tree - no dataset photos, no weight changes derived from them).
These exposed and fixed: wrong shoulder geometry, an edge gain that zeroed
medium-contrast edges, a too-gentle skin-rule slope, and missing small-face
scales. Current synthetic behaviour: frontal/back/face-only persons fire; wood
wall and clutter stay silent; idealised horse and tree cartoons still produce
false positives and grey/very small persons are missed - the known weak points
on real photos.
