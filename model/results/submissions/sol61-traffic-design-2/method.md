# Fixed geometric traffic-sign network

The network extracts luminance, local Gaussian high-pass contrast, saturated red/blue/yellow opponent signals, and bounded dark-stroke features using fixed convolutions, channel normalization, and ReLU operations. Dense matched-filter branches compare those features with idealized signs drawn analytically from circles, polygons, arcs, and handwritten stroke descriptions of numerals, vehicles, people, and arrows. Each symbol hypothesis shares its position, scale, and aspect ratio with a colour/shape gate; max pooling selects a class's best hypothesis, and a fixed readout places its score in the corresponding one of 43 outputs.

All weights and biases are generated directly from formulas and hand-chosen rules in `build.py`. No training images, image descriptors, class averages, fitted coefficients, external fonts, pretrained weights, or data files are used to construct them. Training examples were viewed for qualitative understanding, and exactly five labeled checks were performed through the supplied runner to identify implementation and scoring-logic problems. The final check obtained 48.63% accuracy on a fixed, class-balanced subset of 1,935 training images; this is not held-out accuracy and does not establish superiority over the comparison systems.

The submitted network has 369 layers and 110,724,185 float32 weight entries, below the 120,000,000-entry limit. Running `python3 build.py OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` writes `network.json` and `weights.npz` using NumPy and the standard library, with no input-file reads or network access.
