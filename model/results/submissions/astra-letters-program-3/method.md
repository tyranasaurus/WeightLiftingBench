The classifier compares each photo with synthetic views of the 24 static ASL handshapes. I specified the finger bends, thumb contacts, palm orientation, and wrist direction by hand. The data-free builder renders tapered finger segments and ellipsoidal palms, varying anatomy, camera angle, crop, and illumination with a fixed seed; every stored descriptor and landmark is computed from those renderings, not from a training photograph.

At prediction time, multiscale edge orientations, finger ridges, local shading, and approximate foreground support give a first score for each pose. The matcher then checks the locations of contours and creases, as well as spaces that should be empty, allowing small displacements. The returned label is the class with the strongest combined evidence. I used the training images for qualitative inspection and the logged, labeled sandbox checks only; there is no fitted model or readout.
