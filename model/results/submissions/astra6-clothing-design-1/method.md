# Hand-designed geometric clothing network

The network constructs soft foreground masks using absolute brightness and per-image contrast, then compares open and morphologically closed silhouettes against analytic garment drawings using normalized correlations and maximum pooling. The drawings are made solely from polygons, ellipses, and bands representing torsos, sleeves, necklines, trouser legs, skirts, bag handles, soles, shoe uppers, and sandal straps; a fixed random seed samples hand-specified variations in proportions and position. Additional hand-written rules measure front fastenings, interior texture, leg separation, skirt flare, footwear enclosure, and empty space between a sole and straps.

`build.py` opens no input files and performs no optimization or training; its weights come entirely from these geometric formulas, fixed selectors, finite-difference filters, and manually specified rule coefficients. Training-data access was limited to a class contact sheet, basic dataset metadata, and five labeled checks through the supplied runner; no training image, measured outline, descriptor, class average, or learned parameter is embedded in the weights. Development proceeded through successive hand-designed corrections rather than a search over scored candidate models.

The final network has 85 layers and 15,165,796 float32 weight entries, below the 120,000,000-entry limit. Its final permitted check achieved **58.65% accuracy on 10,000 training examples not used in the preceding four checks**, corresponding to a normalized score of approximately **0.5406** on that check alone. These are not the hidden evaluation images; hidden-set accuracy and performance relative to the two comparison systems are unknown. The principal remaining limitation is distinguishing shirts, pullovers, and coats with similar silhouettes.
