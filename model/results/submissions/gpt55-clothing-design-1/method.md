I built a fully hand-specified network.  It uses three kinds of fixed, geometric evidence: (1) a dense bank of silhouette templates for each clothing class, produced from simple analytic shapes such as trapezoids, capsules, ellipses, polygons and blurred masks; (2) regional brightness and contrast features for broad facts such as two trouser legs with a dark center gap, shoes concentrated near the bottom, bags having a compact body/handle, and boots having a shaft; and (3) a small fixed edge-filter branch with Sobel-like and Laplacian-like filters followed by a few edge templates.  The only numbers in the weights come from these formulas and hand-chosen constants in build.py, not from fitted statistics, patches, labels, or image measurements.

The final score is an elementwise sum of the silhouette-template score, the region-feature score, hinge-style hand rules, and edge-template score.  During development I used the allowed labeled checks only to see broad failure modes (for example, over-favoring bags or boots) and then adjusted hand-set class calibrations and branch strengths by reasoning about the shapes, not by fitting or optimizing weights.
