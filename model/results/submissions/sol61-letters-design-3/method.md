# Data-free anatomical ASL network

The network uses analytic Gaussian-derivative convolutions, ReLUs, pooling, and normalization to extract unsigned edge orientations, signed boundary-normal flow, and finger-width ridge responses. Dense layers compare these features with explicitly programmed geometric descriptions of the 24 static ASL letters. The hand descriptions combine palm ellipses, tapered finger segments, thumb opposition, finger gaps, and schematic creases; a boundary-integral calculation accounts for cancellation of edges separated by less than the optical blur. Both signs of foreground/background contrast are allowed, and max pooling over fixed hand proportions, rotations, scales, aspect ratios, and translations produces the 24 class scores.

Every weight is computed from formulas and hand-written general anatomical constants in `build.py`. The builder reads no files and uses no training images, image-derived descriptors, label statistics, external weights, optimizer, or fitted readout. Labeled image montages were inspected for qualitative understanding only. Network evaluation is performed exclusively through the official labeled checker, within its five-check allowance. The geometric alternatives are all retained within the submitted network, rather than selected using per-image performance.

Rebuild with `python3 build.py OUTPUT_DIRECTORY`. The submitted network has 113,981,984 float32 weights and produces 24 scores. All five official labeled checks were used: the first four on a fixed balanced sample of 1,536 training images, and the final check on all 27,455 training images. Final full-training accuracy was 27.25%; no weights were changed after that check. This is a hand-designed baseline, and the check does not establish accuracy on the unseen scoring set or superiority to the other answers.
