The network draws its shape filters from geometric models: for example, a car has two separated tires below a glazed cabin, while a sailing boat has a tapered hull below a triangular sail. It matches the models against oriented gradients, signed fine-scale contours, and coarse opponent-color maps, pooling over pose, size, reflection, and light/dark material assignments. A second branch measures fuzzy RGB colors, oriented lines, fine texture, and dark landmark arrangements in different parts of the image; hand-written rules combine cues such as grass around a warm-colored animal or horizontal edges over blue water. A rendered-scene branch compares coarse color layout against drawings with symbolic materials (such as rubber, glass, tan fur, foliage, and ocean) under different illuminants. The final scores combine these comparisons with the contour and rule votes. All weights are generated from formulas, drawings, and explicit rules in `build.py`, with a fixed seed for pose variation and no parameters estimated from photographs.
