# Method

**Architecture.** The network has no learned parts; it is a template matcher.
- `norm` the image.
- Fixed convolutions built from formulas:
  - a Gaussian blur, which gives an intensity channel;
  - derivative-of-Gaussian filters at 4 orientations, each taken with both signs, passed through ReLU, then summed and blurred. This gives 4 edge-energy channels that don't depend on whether the hand is lighter or darker than the background.
- A second `norm` on these channels.
- Template convolutions, kernel 28×28 with padding 3, so every template is tried at every shift of up to ±3 px. The score is edge correlation plus the absolute value of intensity correlation: relu(+t) + relu(−t), which tolerates hands darker than the background.
- Max-pool over shifts, then over 48 pose variants per letter. That gives 24 scores.

**Where the weights come from.** Every number is generated by build.py from formulas and a parametric 2D hand renderer I wrote:
- The renderer draws a palm (superellipse), finger capsules with joints, thumb, wrist and forearm, with outline darkening for creases between parts.
- Each letter has a layout typed from general knowledge of ASL handshapes as seen from the front, for a right hand. For example: B is four fingers up with the thumb across the palm; L is index up with the thumb out to the right; G is the index pointing right with the thumb parallel; F is three spread fingers plus an index–thumb ring; fists use curled-finger stubs.
- The 48 variants per letter come from random pose jitter with a fixed seed: finger angles and lengths, rotation ±10°, scale, and how much wrist is in the crop.
- Each render is passed through the same fixed filters in numpy, then standardised to become a template.

No training image, label statistic or fitting of any kind went into the weights. The training images were only viewed as PNGs to understand what the signs look like.

**Checks.** Four checks ran on all 27,455 training images: 39.3%, 33.0%, 34.2% and 38.7%; a fifth run ran out of memory before finishing. The submitted network is the check-1 configuration (39.3%); build.py reproduces its weights exactly.
