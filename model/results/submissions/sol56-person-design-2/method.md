I built a four-scale, sliding-window shape detector from analytic filters and generic anatomical models. The first layer contains Gaussian directional derivatives plus smoothed luminance and colour-opponent filters; per-image channel normalization makes their magnitudes comparatively insensitive to lighting. The next banks encode hand-constructed outlines of heads, shoulders, torsos, articulated arms and legs, upper bodies, faces, and small groups, with several generic proportions and poses. Conservative counter-templates encode generic quadrupeds, pointed-ear animal faces, plates, and vehicles, all common non-person subjects in everyday scenes. Each bank scans at a different effective size, spatial max pooling supplies translation tolerance, and fixed evidence votes combine the related human and counter-evidence templates.

All constants are generated in `build.py` from Gaussian and geometric formulas with no data access, fitting, copied pixels, or image-derived measurements. The formulas are deterministic and reproduce both the architecture and float32 weights.
