The network measures sky-subtracted light and red–blue contrast in Gaussian apertures on concentric rings around the catalogue centre. Diametrically opposed apertures estimate the axis ratio at several brightness levels; changes in that ratio, radial concentration, and angular Fourier phase help distinguish spheroids, bars, spiral arms, and edge-on disks. Separate branches look for a second resolved nucleus across a brightness saddle and for one-sided light that stays connected to the galaxy over several radii. The final ReLU layers implement hand-written morphology rules. All weights are generated from these formulas and geometric constants by `build.py`, without reading any images, labels, or other input files.
