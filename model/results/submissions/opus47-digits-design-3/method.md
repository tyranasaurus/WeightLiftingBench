# Method

## Architecture
A single 28x28 convolution followed by max-pool per class:

1. `match`: `conv` with 480 kernels (shape `(480, 1, 28, 28)`), stride 1, no padding. Output `(480, 1, 1)` — one dot-product per template.
2. `r1`: `reshape` to `(1, 10, 48)` — 10 classes, 48 templates each.
3. `pick`: `maxpool` with window `(1, 48)` and stride `(1, 48)` — the best template response per class.
4. `out`: `reshape` to `(10,)` — the ten class scores.

Total weights: 376,800.

## How the weights are produced
The weights are hand-designed digit templates, generated by rendering
parametric stroke paths onto a 28x28 canvas. For each class, I describe the
digit as a list of pen paths (lines and elliptical arcs) in a normalized
design space, where ±0.25 maps to the ±10-pixel extent of the 20x20 digit box
(pixel centre is at (13.5, 13.5)). Strokes are drawn as Gaussian-profile
polylines using a chosen pen sigma. For each digit, I use 48 templates built
from the product of four parameters:

- 4 style variants per digit, capturing common handwriting styles (plain,
  with-serif, angular, rounded, etc.);
- 4 slant values (−0.20 to +0.18), tilting each template left or right;
- 3 pen widths (sigma 0.030, 0.055, 0.085 in design units).

After rendering, every template is zero-meaned and L2-normalized, so each is
a unit-norm 28x28 pattern. Max-pool over the 48 templates gives one
cosine-similarity-like score per class. The class with the largest score is
the prediction. All numbers come from the rendering formulas — no training
data, labels, or image statistics are used.
