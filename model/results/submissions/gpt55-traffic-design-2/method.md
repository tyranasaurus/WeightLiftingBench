The network is a hand-built template matcher for German traffic-sign geometry. It normalizes the input per channel, keeps both raw and normalized pooled images, and applies a small bank of fixed 1x1 colour/opponent filters; dense layers then compare these feature maps with analytic templates for circles, triangles, diamonds, octagons, arrows, digits and common pictograms.

All weights are generated by `/app/out/build.py` from formula-drawn sign models, not from fitting or copying training images. For each class the builder renders variants at several hand-chosen scales, small offsets, small rotations, and simple lighting changes, masks the relevant sign interior, normalizes the templates, and writes them as dense correlation filters. The final layer reshapes the per-template scores to `(43, templates)` and max-pools over templates to get one score per class.

I used the allowed checks only to identify broad failure modes such as overly generic blank-circle matches and poor alignment tolerance, then adjusted the hand-coded templates, feature weights, and class biases manually.
