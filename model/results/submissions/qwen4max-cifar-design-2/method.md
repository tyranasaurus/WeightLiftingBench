# Hand-designed CIFAR-10 network (no training, no data-derived weights)

## Architecture
The block set has no `concat`, so parallel branches are merged by **adding their
10-way score vectors** (`add` of three `dense -> (10,)` heads). 26 layers,
14,590 weights.

* **S branch (colour layout).** `1x1 conv` colour primitives (R,G,B, luminance,
  green/blue/red dominance, pairwise channel differences) -> `relu` -> `1x1 conv`
  forming saturation and luminance hinges -> `relu` -> `1x1 conv` with biases that
  build *semantic colour channels* as linear thresholds (soft ANDs):
  `sky` = bright AND blue, `water` = blue AND not-bright, `veg` = green-dominant,
  `gray` = low-saturation mid-luminance, `white` = bright AND unsaturated,
  `dark`, `brown` = warm R>G>B. -> `relu` -> a fixed saturating hinge
  `(relu(x)-relu(x-cap))/cap` bounding every channel to [0,1] -> `avgpool 8x8`
  (4x4 grid) -> `dense(10)`.
* **E branch (texture / structure).** `7x7 conv` Gabor bank (6 orientations x
  2 scales, +/- signs for absolute value) plus a fine Laplacian, a dark-ring
  (wheel) and a dark-vertical-bar (leg) detector, all on luminance -> `relu` ->
  `1x1 conv` combining +/- pairs into orientation energy, plus `FUR` = mean of the
  six fine-scale energies (isotropic fine texture) -> `relu` -> the same [0,1]
  hinge -> two heads: `avgpool 4x4 -> dense(10)` (layout/texture) and
  `maxpool 4x4 -> dense(10)` (peak shape presence).
* **Output.** `add` of the three score vectors -> `(10,)`.

## How the weights were produced
Everything is generated by `build.py` from the code alone (it reads no files):
* Gabor / Laplacian kernels from their analytic formulas, normalised to a fixed
  gain; ring and bar kernels from hand-written ellipse/Gaussian shape models of a
  wheel and a leg; colour thresholds and gains chosen by hand from general
  knowledge of sky/water/foliage/road/fur colours.
* The readouts are a **hand-written rule table**: for each class, a list of
  (channel, spatial region, magnitude) entries over the 4x4 grid (regions are
  normalised masks: top / bottom / centre / mid / all). Examples: ship = blue
  water below + horizontal horizon + pale hull; airplane = bright sky above +
  pale smooth fuselage + horizontal wings; frog = green smooth centre;
  cat/dog = centre fur texture gated by negatives for green/blue/saturated
  context; vehicles = smooth (negative fine texture) + wheels/box edges.
  No statistic of the training images or labels is used anywhere.

## Checks (5 allowed, all used; logged in checks.log)
1. 2000 imgs, 0.1275 — degenerate: everything defaulted to *airplane*; sky/water/
   white thresholds too strict and a raw-brightness bonus favoured airplane.
2. 2000 imgs, 0.1800 — *bird* and *cat* never predicted: cat's rules were a subset
   of dog's (domination); broad ring/bar detectors gave vehicles free points.
3. 2000 imgs, 0.1820 — *horse* became the default: ring/bar/brown too broad and
   the Laplacian texture cue too weak; added the reliable `FUR` Gabor texture.
4. 2000 imgs, 0.1595 — *dog* dominated cat by a small margin; made fur-weight
   differences large and gave cat/bird unique signatures.
5. 5000 imgs, 0.1792 — *cat* defaulted on any textured centre; fixed by gating the
   fur classes with strong negatives for green/blue/saturated context.

Each check was used only to detect and repair structural degeneracy (a class that
can never win, or a universal default), not to fit weights to labels. After the
fifth check the remaining fixes (context gating for fur classes, overcast-sky and
tarmac cues for airplane, wing-vs-box orientation) were made by reasoning about
the class prototypes alone; the final weights were not re-scored on images.
`check_network.py` reports the network usable and `check_rebuild.py` confirms
`build.py` reproduces `network.json` and `weights.npz` exactly without any data.
