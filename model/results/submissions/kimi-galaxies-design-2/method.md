The network is a hand-built morphology matcher, not a trained model. A three-band luminance image is compressed with a fixed piecewise-linear square-root tone curve, then examined at three central/full-field scales. Each scale is normalized per image and correlated with 512 analytic templates per class: smooth elliptical Sérsic-like profiles, thin disks with and without a separate bulge, logarithmic spiral arms with different winding and bar geometries, asymmetric clumpy systems, and two-component mergers. Template parameters and position angles are generated deterministically from general geometric models; exact max-pooling over variants gives rotation and scale tolerance.

A small fixed Gaussian-aperture g-minus-z term encodes the general colour distinction between spheroids and blue spiral/distorted systems. In addition, positive second moments at four intensity levels are projected onto sixteen axes and max/min pooled over angle. Capped comparisons of the maximum and minimum moments provide rotation-invariant thin-disk and round-core evidence for distinguishing the two edge-on classes and suppressing overly thin smooth matches. No training images, image statistics, labels, regression, optimization, or learned parameters are used by `build.py`; all weights follow from the formulas and the fixed random seed in its source.
