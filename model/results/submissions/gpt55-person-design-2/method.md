The submitted network is a hand-written multiscale template detector, not a trained model.  It first averages the 96x96 RGB image to three resolutions (2x, 4x and 8x pooling), then applies formula-derived colour, brightness and oriented-edge convolutions.  Small 1x1 convolutions combine those primitive responses into hand-specified cues such as skin-colour margins, dark/bright contrast, vertical and horizontal boundary energy, warm/green/blue material, and generic object strokes.

At each scale, fixed convolutional templates vote for person-like arrangements: face ellipses with darker hair/eye bands, heads over shoulders, upright bodies, diagonal limb/body patterns, and repeated small heads for groups.  The template numbers were typed from general knowledge about people in everyday photos and simple geometric patterns; no weight was fit to, copied from, or derived from any training image or label.  Max pooling lets a person occur anywhere in the photo, while average pooling gives weaker support for repeated small people/crowds.

I looked at random montages of training images only to understand the task distribution.  Five labeled checks were used as allowed; the final architecture was then made more conservative from those qualitative lessons and from visual inspection, and was verified with check_network.py and check_rebuild.py.
