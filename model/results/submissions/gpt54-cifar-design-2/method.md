I built a hand-written two-branch network with no fitting or gradient descent. One branch uses 1x1 colour detectors followed by 8x8 pooling and a dense readout to score coarse scene cues such as sky, water, road, grass, bright regions and dark regions. The other branch converts the image to grayscale, normalises it, splits bright and dark contrast into two channels, downsamples to 16x16, and applies many fixed 9x9 silhouette filters for airplanes, cars, birds, cat faces/bodies, deer, dogs, frogs, horses, ships and trucks; max-pooling makes these template matches position-tolerant.

All weights were produced directly in build.py from typed geometric shape formulas (ellipses, rectangles and line segments) and hand-set constants based on general knowledge of the classes, not from any statistics of the training images or labels. I inspected training images only to understand what kinds of objects and backgrounds appear, then adjusted the architecture and constants by hand across the five allowed scorer checks.
