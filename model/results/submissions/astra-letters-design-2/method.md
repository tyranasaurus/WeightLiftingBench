I model each static letter using a tapered wrist, a palm pad, and articulated, tapered fingers. The pose specifies which fingers are extended, curled, crossed, or touching, and which surface faces the camera. The builder projects and shades these shapes under fixed-seed variations of hand proportions, joint angles, wrist rotation, lighting, and crop. It converts those entirely synthetic projections into normalized, orientation-selective filters.

At inference the network normalizes the photograph and measures directed edges, spots, and local shading. It attenuates very strong responses and pools evidence from nearby pixels. Shared convolution kernels measure agreement with the modeled poses at nine translations, allowing fingers and palm to align slightly differently. Each letter's score sums the strongest match in each of eight pose groups, so one accidental match cannot dominate the result. No training photograph, training-set statistic, fitted parameter, or external asset is used by the builder.
