The classifier estimates the sky in each image, locates the central nucleus, and measures the shape, orientation, and centroid of successive equal-brightness contours. It looks for a second resolved nucleus with a common light envelope, uses asymmetry to identify disturbances, and compares the vertical thickness, central concentration, and color of an edge-on disk with its outer regions. For other disks it samples deprojected elliptical rings: a straight inner elongation suggests a bar, while angular Fourier phases, arm–interarm contrast, and winding in log radius distinguish spiral patterns. The filters and decision rules are hand-written from geometric and photometric considerations; I inspected labeled contact sheets and used five disjoint labeled checks, without fitting parameters or storing training images or descriptors. Each image is processed independently, with no randomness or external constants.
