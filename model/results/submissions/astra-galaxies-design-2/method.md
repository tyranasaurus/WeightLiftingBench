The network is a hand-built galaxy photometer. It estimates the sky separately in each band, samples the central galaxy on polar grids, and measures the shape and asymmetry of equal-brightness contours. It tests for winding arms with phase-sensitive filters on inclined disks, and for mergers with paired brightness peaks that have both a saddle between them and extended light around them. Piecewise-linear rules combine these measurements with color, concentration, and the change in flattening from core to outskirts to produce ten class scores. All weights are generated from analytic kernels, geometric sampling grids, and hand-written rules; no image-derived weights or fitted parameters are used.

The final labeled check was 660/1,250 correct (52.8%) on a random subset of the official training set disjoint from the earlier check subset. This is not an evaluation on the hidden test images.
