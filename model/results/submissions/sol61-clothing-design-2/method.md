# Data-free analytic clothing network

The network has 54 layers and 6,072,623 float32 weight entries. A fixed foreground threshold, max/average pooling, and per-image normalization produce a coarse silhouette. A dense layer correlates it against 3,072 procedural shape models per class, followed by max pooling to take the strongest match for each class. These models are generated from polygons, ellipses, and line segments describing generic clothing anatomy: bodies and sleeves, bodices and skirts, waistbands and separated legs, soles and uppers, and bags with handle loops. A fixed random seed samples manually specified proportions and small pose/centering variations; it never samples training data.

Parallel hand-written branches supply bounded evidence for central closures, leg gaps, shoulders and sleeves, sparse/open footwear, thin straps, and enclosed holes. All filters, regional masks, thresholds, shape ranges, and readout coefficients are explicit formulas or manually chosen constants in `build.py`. There is no gradient descent, fitting routine, empirical class template, image bank, or data access in the builder. It reads no files and writes `network.json` and `weights.npz` to its argument directory.

I viewed a labeled image overview and made five sequential diagnostic checks, revising general anatomical rules by hand rather than fitting coefficients or searching variants. The final check achieved **63.49% on the first 10,000 official training images**, not on the unseen scoring set. Its normalized score on that check is approximately 0.5943. Confusions among shirts, pullovers, and coats remain substantial; no performance claim is made for either comparison baseline or the hidden set.
