The classifier measures each galaxy independently. It subtracts the local sky, finds the central light peak, and follows rays out to several equal-brightness contours to measure shape, asymmetry, and faint tidal extensions. It tests for a merger by looking for a second resolved nucleus joined to the first by a light bridge; for edge-on disks, it compares vertical light profiles near and away from the nucleus to detect a bulge. In an elliptical log-polar view of the disk, angular Fourier modes measure spiral winding, while paired straight ridges in red light provide evidence for a stellar bar.

I wrote the measurements and decision rules by hand, using labeled galleries for visual inspection and five labeled checks through the supplied runner. No model was trained, and no training images, descriptors, or class statistics are included. The standalone build script emits the NumPy source directly and requires no constants file. The final check classified 577 of 1,000 training-set examples correctly; it was not a test on the hidden set.
