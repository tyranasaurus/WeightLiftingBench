The network normalizes the image, applies an analytic Gaussian blur, and computes four fixed Sobel/diagonal derivative channels. It takes their absolute values for contrast-polarity invariance and pools them to a 14 x 14 spatial map; a parallel pooled brightness map is also normalized. The readout performs matched filtering against procedural geometric drawings of the 24 ASL hand shapes, built from palm ellipses, wrist and finger capsules, thumb segments, and anatomical crease lines. Nine general pose/scale/translation variants and both brightness polarities are compared for each letter, followed by a maximum over variants. Every number is generated from formulas and hand-written shape rules in `build.py`; no training image, label statistic, fitted parameter, or stored image descriptor is used. Matched filters are normalized to have unit response to their own synthetic drawings so that classes with larger contours do not receive artificially larger scores.
