# Method

**Task.** Person / no-person on 96x96 COCO photos. All weights are hand-designed from
general knowledge of how people appear in photographs and from closed-form filter
formulas. `build.py` reads no files; every number is generated by its own code.

**Architecture** (216 layers, 25,794 weights, ~3.4k non-zero):

1. **Skin-colour map.** A 1x1 conv + ReLU + 1x1 conv implements seven hand-written
   linear chromaticity rules, each turned into a 0..1 ramp by `relu(x)-relu(x-1)`:
   hue below ~33 deg (`0.55R-G+0.45B>0`), redness `R-G`, saturation ceiling `B>0.36R`,
   saturation floor `R-B`, `G>B`, `G<1.42B`, and a luminance window. A weighted soft-AND
   of the ramps gives a skin likelihood. The rules are homogeneous in RGB so they
   survive exposure changes; the `G<1.42B` rule is what separates skin from dark wood,
   terracotta courts and brown fur, and the hue rule rejects sand, beige walls,
   cardboard and yellow food. I checked the rule against a synthetic palette of skin
   tones and common confusers (no training data involved).

2. **Pyramid.** Eight scales with cell sizes 1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16 px (1.5x steps, so face
   widths 3.4-54 px are covered). Skin and texture are area-averaged down; luminance is
   averaged down and a *scale-matched* dark-spot map (`2.5*relu(3x3 mean - centre)`) is
   recomputed at each level, so eyes stay visible at every scale.

3. **Four formula templates per scale**, all built from Gaussians / ellipses / bands:
   * `blob` - difference of Gaussians: compact skin region surrounded by non-skin;
   * `head` - blob + "skin below, hair above" asymmetry + vertical elongation
     (people stand upright and the photos are squashed to square; animals, floors and
     food lie horizontally);
   * `face` - skin ellipse with a non-skin ring, **plus eyes measured as two holes in
     the skin map** with skin-coloured nose bridge and cheeks between/below them, plus
     a small luminance dark-band term. Measuring the eye pattern on the skin map means
     it can only fire where skin actually is;
   * `tblob` - blob minus local |Laplacian| texture (fur, food and wood grain are
     textured, skin is smooth).
   Responses are averaged over 2x2 neighbourhoods before the global max, so a single
   noise peak cannot win.

4. **Globals.** Skin fraction, densest 12/24/48 px skin windows, top/bottom-half skin
   balance, and colourfulness (`mean|R-G|+|G-B|`, exactly 0 for the black-and-white
   photos, which have R=G=B).

5. **Readout.** Because there is no concat op, each pooled measurement is scattered
   into one feature vector by a selection-matrix `dense`, and the vectors are summed
   with `add`. Four hand-written dense/ReLU stages then implement a rule table:
   per-scale hinges on blob/head/face/texture, a total "face support" term, and gates
   that subtract large skin masses, bottom-heavy skin (floors), and scattered
   skin-coloured clutter **unless a face supports them**. A separate luminance-only
   face branch, switched off by any colourfulness, recovers people in black-and-white
   photos. Thresholds and weights were chosen by hand from the geometry of the
   templates, not fitted.

**Checks used.** 4 of the 5 allowed runs: 58.5%, 56.3%, 57.6%, 56.9%. They diagnosed
two real defects that I fixed analytically: the eye term originally dominated the face
channel and could reach threshold with no skin present (firing on plain texture and
falsely supplying "face support" that disabled the gates), and the skin soft-AND was
too brittle, so real skin pixels scored far below 1. The submitted weights are the
corrected design (check 4): eyes are holes in the skin map, the luminance eye term is
capped at 0.8, and the skin ramp saturates so mild rule deficits still count as skin.

**Honest status.** Accuracy stayed in the 0.56-0.59 band across all four operating
points I tried; the hand-designed skin/shape evidence is only weakly discriminative at
96x96, and I did not reach the accuracy I was aiming for. The submitted answer is the
last, structurally-corrected design rather than the highest-scoring earlier one,
because the earlier score came from a face channel that fired on non-skin texture.
`check_rebuild.py` passes with max relative difference 0.0.
