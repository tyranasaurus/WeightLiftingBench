# Data-free geometric ASL network

The network standardizes image contrast, then uses fixed Sobel projections, rectification, channelwise normalization, and averaging to form unsigned oriented edges, signed gradients, and coarse intensity. A convolutional bank scores manually specified geometric models of the 24 static ASL letters. Each model is assembled from palm ellipses, rounded finger segments, opposing thumb segments, and weak crease/shading formulas; analytic variants cover finger separation, bulk, foreshortening, camera shear, scale, in-plane rotation, reflection, lighting, and contrast polarity. Max pooling chooses a nearby translation and then the highest-scoring symbolic pose for each letter.

Every weight is generated by `build.py` from these formulas, manually chosen constants, and a fixed random seed for small anatomical variations. The generator opens no data files and performs no optimization, parameter fitting, class averaging, or image/descriptor copying. Training images were viewed only as an allowed montage; five labeled checks used the supplied runner. Check results informed qualitative design revisions, not numerical fitting or class-specific score calibration.

The final architecture has 23 layers and 76,382,572 float32 weight entries, below the 120,000,000-entry limit. Its last permitted check obtained 26.04% accuracy on a class-balanced 1,536-image training subset (64 examples per class); this is not a held-out accuracy estimate. The five checks were 20.44%, 28.12%, 29.82%, and 30.21% on a 768-image subset, followed by the final 26.04% on the larger subset. No held-out images were available, and superiority over the comparison methods has not been established.

Rebuild with `python3 build.py OUTPUT_DIRECTORY`. The only required generated artifacts are `network.json` and `weights.npz`; both are deterministic.
