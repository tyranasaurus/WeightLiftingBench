I used a fixed analytic front-end and a bank of procedural silhouette models. The input is downsampled to 14×14, then a five-channel convolution computes intensity, local average, horizontal/vertical Sobel responses, and a Laplacian. A dense layer compares those features with 256 hand-generated variants per class, and max-pooling selects the strongest variant before returning ten scores.

Every template is generated in `build.py` from idealized geometric primitives chosen from general clothing knowledge: tapered torsos and sleeves, trouser legs and a crotch gap, dress skirts, coat openings/lapels, sandal straps, sneaker soles, bag handles, and boot shafts. Variants use a fixed random seed and bounded, hand-chosen ranges for generic pose/proportion diversity. No training image, crop, patch, descriptor, class statistic, fitting procedure, or learned parameter is used in constructing the weights. Five allowed checks were used only to diagnose broad score behavior; the final small class offsets are hand-set semantic calibration rather than fitted values.
