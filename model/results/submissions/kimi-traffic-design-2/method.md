The network is a hand-drawn, multi-scale template correlator. A 1x1 convolution converts RGB to grey level and red/blue colour-opponent channels; 2x2 averaging followed by per-channel standardization provides illumination robustness. Separate convolutions compare this 16x16 representation with normalized outer-sign and inner-pictogram templates. A second greyscale branch at 32x32 preserves narrow strokes for numerals and selected detailed symbols. For each class, max pooling selects the best of twelve hand-chosen scale, aspect-ratio, and translation variants, producing the 43 scores.

Every weight is generated by `build.py` from analytic geometry and fixed colour constants: rings, discs, triangles, diamonds, an octagon, geometric digits, arrows, vehicles, people, and other standard sign pictograms. Template kernels are explicitly centred and L2-normalized to implement normalized correlation. No training image, label statistic, fitted parameter, stored descriptor, or optimization procedure is used. The five permitted checks informed architectural choices such as separating outer and inner evidence and avoiding a generic blank-interior response; no further evaluation was run after the final targeted fine-detail selection.
