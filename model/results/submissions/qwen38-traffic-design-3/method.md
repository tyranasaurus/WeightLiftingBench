# Method

**Architecture.** A hand-designed, fully interpretable template-matching network.
Front end: a 3x3 colour-opponent conv (luminance, redness, blueness, yellowness, greenness),
a per-channel `norm` (makes responses invariant to the large lighting variation), a 1x1 conv
that turns the normalised channels into five non-negative "paint" maps (red paint, blue paint,
yellow paint, white paint, dark paint) with hand-chosen sigma thresholds, a relu, and a small
3x3 smoothing conv.
Read-out: four correlation banks of hand-drawn sign templates (conv = correlation with the
template as kernel), each followed by relu (threshold) and a global maxpool (position
invariance): a 26x26 bank of whole-sign family templates (red ring, red disc + bar, red
triangle up/down, octagon outline, yellow diamond thin/thick border, blue disc, white disc with
dark rim and anti-red), a 16x16 bank of white-on-blue arrow templates, an 18x18 bank of digit
strings ("20"..."120"), and a 16x16 bank of dark glyphs (vehicles, slash, exclamation mark,
bends, double bend, bump, skid, narrowing, worker, traffic light, pedestrian, children, bicycle,
snowflake, deer, STOP letters, bar). Glyph templates carry a tight white/blue halo context so
they only fire on the sign face. Four dense layers implement a hand-written rule table
(class = family evidence + glyph evidence, with hand-picked negatives for mutually exclusive
families) and an `add` produces the 43 class scores.

**Weights.** No training, no fitting, no statistics of the training images or labels entered the
weights. Every weight is generated by code in `build.py` from formulas and shape models typed
from general knowledge of German traffic signs (StVO): rings, triangles, octagon, diamonds and
discs from distance functions; arrows, vehicles, figures and slashes from line-segment strokes;
digits from 5x7 stroke bitmaps scaled to size. Thresholds were set by hand from the general
contrast structure of daylight photos (paint stands ~1-2 normalised sigmas above the image mean);
a couple of training images per class were viewed only to confirm which sign variant each class
uses (e.g. slash direction on the "end of" signs, orientation of the double bend).
