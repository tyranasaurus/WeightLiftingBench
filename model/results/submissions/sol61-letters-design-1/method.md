# Data-free anatomical ASL network

The network applies image normalization, four analytic derivative-of-Gaussian orientations, absolute-value rectification, fixed edge saturation, local max pooling, and per-orientation normalization. A fixed dense layer correlates these features against 4,800 procedural hand poses per class; a final max pool chooses the strongest pose match for each of the 24 classes. The network has 90,432,344 float32 parameters, below the 120,000,000 limit.

Every matching template is generated from a three-dimensional hand model written in `build.py`: an ellipsoidal palm, capsule fingers with explicit joint flexion and abduction, and an opposable thumb. ASL configurations specify extended, curled, crossed, or contacting fingers. A fixed random seed supplies anatomical, viewpoint, framing, and illumination variation. Analytic ray intersections and smoothed surface normals produce synthetic surfaces, whose deterministic edge descriptors become the dense weights. No training images, image measurements, class statistics, fitted parameters, downloaded models, or optimization are used in the weight generator, which opens no input files.

Training examples were viewed as contact sheets, and five official labeled checks were used to diagnose the manually designed model. Their accuracies were 26.11%, 27.77%, 30.48%, 31.95%, and 30.97%. The submission is the fifth design, not a post-check selection or ensemble of earlier versions. Its final training-check accuracy is 30.97%; hidden-set accuracy and superiority to the comparison systems are unknown. Closely related closed-fist configurations and some foreshortened signs remain significant limitations.
