The network is a data-free, hand-designed template matcher.  It first normalizes the input image per channel, then computes a small bank of fixed analytic 3x3 filters (identity/blur/Sobel/Laplacian-like edge filters) with ReLU, 2x2 average pooling, and normalization.  In parallel it keeps the normalized raw image.

The dense template layers compare those raw and edge features with generated ASL sign prototypes.  The prototypes are not copied or fitted from the training set: `build.py` draws them procedurally from simple geometric primitives (ellipses and capsules for palm, wrist, fingers, curled fingers, thumb, rings/holes) for each of the 24 static ASL letters.  It includes hand-coded pose variation (translation, scale, rotation), both contrast polarities, and mirrored variants where useful.  Hollow/crease regions are given opposite sign so holes in O/F/D-like signs are rewarded only when they look like background rather than filled fist.

For each class, the network reshapes all template scores and max-pools over its generated variants.  A final identity dense layer emits the 24 class scores.  No training data, labels, image statistics, regression, or optimization are read by `build.py`; every weight is generated from formulas and constants in the source.
