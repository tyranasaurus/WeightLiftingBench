I built a hand-designed two-branch network for CIFAR-like 32x32 colour images. The first convolution uses fixed Gaussian-smoothed colour-opponent filters (blue, green, red, yellow, brown, cyan, bright, dark) plus oriented edge and Laplacian filters. After ReLU, one branch uses average pooling to make a direct class score from coarse absolute colour-and-layout evidence such as sky above, water below, road, green foreground, warm textured animal centre, and boxy vehicle structure. The second branch uses max pooling, channelwise normalization, and a bank of manually written prototype detectors for typical spatial arrangements of airplanes, ships, cars, trucks, birds, cats, dogs, deer, frogs, and horses; a final dense layer sums groups of those prototype activations into class logits.

All weights were written directly from general knowledge about the classes and from hand-crafted formulas for filters and masks. I looked at training images only to understand the task visually, then adjusted the architecture and constants by hand; no fitting, optimization, regression, averaging, clustering, or gradient descent was used.
