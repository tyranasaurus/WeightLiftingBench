# Hand-designed traffic-sign network

The network uses a piecewise-linear logarithm of RGB, analytic red/blue/yellow colour contrasts, and fixed circle, triangle, and diamond masks to recognize sign families. Fixed bilinear sampling matrices examine 108 geometric hypotheses; analytic removal of constant and linear illumination gradients and runtime channel normalization make the interior-symbol comparisons less sensitive to lighting. Convolutional matched filters are generated from hand-written geometric artwork for digits, arrows, vehicles, people, and warning symbols, with fixed scale, aspect, rotation, and subpixel variants; max pooling combines the hypotheses, and hand-written semantic rules combine symbol and colour evidence. Empty interiors use an explicit absolute-deviation test rather than a constant symbol score.

Every weight is generated by `build.py` from formulas and generic sign descriptions, without reading any files, using an optimizer, or copying or computing weight statistics from training images. Gram–Schmidt in the builder only orthogonalizes the three analytic illumination basis functions over ideal geometric masks; it does not process image data. Training images were viewed as contact sheets, and the five permitted labelled runner calls were used to diagnose design failures; no other execution of the network or its layers on images was used.

The final network has 62 layers and 89,505,733 float32 weight entries. Its fifth and final permitted check achieved 58.43% accuracy on a fixed, label-independent subset of 3,921 training images (every tenth image). This is not a held-out accuracy estimate or evidence of beating either comparison system; warning pictograms and cancellation signs remain substantial weaknesses.
