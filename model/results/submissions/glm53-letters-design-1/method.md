# Method

## Architecture (13-layer network, ~15.7M weights)

1. **`norm`** on the input image: per-image standardisation removes brightness/contrast variation.
2. **Two parallel `conv` layers** (`cp`, `cn`, 13×13, stride 1, pad 6, 38 channels each) holding the same
   38 formula filters with opposite signs: 12 anisotropic Gabor *ridge* filters + 12 Gabor *edge* filters
   (15° orientation steps), 6 coarser ridges, 4 very coarse bars (overall hand direction), 2
   difference-of-Gaussian blobs and 2 annulus (ring) filters for O/C. All filters are analytic formulas,
   L2-normalised, zero-mean.
3. **`relu` on each conv, then `add`**: relu(r)+relu(−r) = |r|, so every channel is polarity-invariant
   (the hand may be lighter or darker than the background).
4. **Two scale paths** from the |response| stack:
   - fine: `maxpool` 2×2 → 38×14×14, `reshape`, `norm` (whole-vector standardisation);
   - coarse: `avgpool` 4×4 → 38×7×7, `reshape`, `norm` (clutter-robust, translation-tolerant).
5. **Generative template banks**: a `dense` layer per path whose rows are feature vectors of *rendered*
   hand poses — 64 fine + 96 coarse pose variants per class — followed by `reshape` to (24, 1, V) and
   `maxpool` over the variant axis, i.e. the class score is the **best template match over poses**.
   The two paths' scores are summed (`add`).
6. **Biases** subtract each class's mean false-alarm level, measured on synthetic probe images only.

## How the weights were produced (no training, no data)

Everything comes from code in `build.py`:

- **Parametric hand renderer** (general anatomy, palm units: palm width 10): palm ellipse, finger and
  thumb capsules with anatomical lengths/widths (index 7.5, middle 8.6, ring 7.8, pinky 5.6, widths
  2.0–2.4, thumb 2.6), wrist stub. Each of the 24 letters is posed from knowledge of the ASL alphabet:
  finger extension angles, curled-finger knuckle bumps, thumb position (side bar for A, across the
  fingers just below the knuckles for B/U/V/W/R/X/S, between the fingers for K/T/M/N, pinched sideways
  pair for G, out to the side for L/Y, ring for O, crescent for C, hook for X, crossed chains for R,
  180°-rotated K/G for P/Q). Poses are jittered over rotation, translation, scale, mirror, contrast,
  edge sharpness and blur to cover the crop/augmentation variability described in the task.
- Each render is pushed through the exact same feature pipeline as the network, standardised,
  optionally block-smoothed, and written as one dense row. Per-class and per-channel/row weighting
  emphasises the discriminative upper half of the frame.
- The coarse path's gain and all 24 class biases are calibrated on **synthetic** probe renders with
  simulated clutter (flat background, occasional strong background edge lines occluded by the hand,
  shading blobs, noise, brightness/contrast jitter) — never on training images or labels.

## Design iterations

The training set was used only for looking (text renderings, class-mean masks, simple statistics) and
for 5 logged `run_network` checks. The first checks showed a collapse onto the "smooth shape" classes
(O/G/H/C); score dumps from a diagnostic run on hand-picked clear images showed that thumb-less
templates matched while every thumb-bearing letter ranked badly, which revealed that my thumb
placements were 4–6 px too low — real thumbs sit just below the knuckle line. All thumb positions were
corrected from anatomy in the final model. (The 5th logged check ran on synthetic renders generated by
`build.py`, not on training images.)

On synthetic test images with realistic clutter the network reaches ~53% (moderate pose scatter) /
~42% (wide pose scatter); on real data the thumb fix could not be re-verified within the 5-run budget.
