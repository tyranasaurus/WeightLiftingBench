The network measures the central galaxy's apparent axis ratio with rotated elliptical filters, and compares its thickness at several surface-brightness levels to distinguish a bulge from a thin disk. In trial disk planes it measures angular Fourier coefficients on logarithmic rings: a bar maintains its orientation across rings, whereas spiral arms change phase. Aperture colors, arm/interarm contrast, half-turn asymmetry, and peaks away from the nucleus help distinguish smooth, star-forming, disturbed, and merging systems. `build.py` constructs the filters from geometry and compiles hand-written measurement and scoring rules into affine layers, pooling, and ReLUs. No weights were fitted to images or labels.
