The classifier compares each photo, in both horizontal orientations, to a library of 11,520 individual templates. I wrote a seeded depth-buffer renderer that builds vehicles from hulls, panels, windows and wheels, and animals from articulated body parts, with different poses, viewpoints, coats, lighting and backgrounds. Each rendering becomes one template: its descriptor records coarse light and shadow, oriented edges, texture, and opponent colors. The predictor compares a photo's foreground, estimated from differences from its border colors and textures, to each rendered silhouette. It combines those matches with appearance distances and hand-written cues such as paired wheels, face symmetry, vegetation, and water. No training photograph was used to create or select a template, coefficient, or rule; no statistics were collected across renderings to set constants.
