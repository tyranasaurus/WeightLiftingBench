The network measures the central galaxy through a sequence of Gaussian apertures, subtracting local sky light and using the light distribution to select an aperture size and estimate its axis ratio and position angle. It measures two- and four-fold angular structure on elliptical rings: an elongated core that ends abruptly favors a bar, while a changing angular phase with radius favors spiral arms. Other branches compare opposite sides of the galaxy, look for a second brightness peak, and measure color and central concentration to distinguish interactions, smooth galaxies, and edge-on disks with or without bulges. The final scores combine these measurements with hand-written, continuous decision rules. All weights come from geometry, analytic filters, arithmetic approximations, and those rules; none were fitted to real or generated images.
