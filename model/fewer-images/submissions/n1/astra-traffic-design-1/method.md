The network first compresses illumination with a piecewise-linear approximation to log intensity, then separates intensity from red and blue colour contrast. One branch matches sign borders; a second subtracts slowly varying light and reads the central pictogram. Some readers require both parts to match at the same pose, while speed-limit readers compare both the whole inscription and its distinguishing digit. Cancellation signs have an additional reader that masks out the common diagonal stripe.

All weights are specified by formulas or by my own geometric drawings in `build.py`: polygons for sign faces, strokes and arcs for digits, and small drawings of vehicles, arrows, and warning symbols. The program varies scale, position, and orientation deterministically and normalizes each template using only its own support. At inference, max pooling selects matching poses and a fixed rule combines their evidence. No training image, external font, or fitted parameter is used by the builder.
