The classifier traces equal-brightness contours around the central galaxy to measure its apparent axis ratio, orientation, and departures from symmetry. It samples the light in elliptical rings and searches for winding spiral arms by following angular Fourier phases across log radius. A fixed-direction inner elongation supports a bar; vertical thickness, central light excess, and a dust lane help distinguish edge-on disks and bulges. A separate test looks for a resolved second nucleus and light connecting it to the first.

I wrote the filters and decision rules by hand from these morphological criteria. Each image supplies its own sky background, noise scale, and geometry. There are no learned weights, stored examples, or generated templates; `build.py` writes the complete NumPy implementation from embedded source without reading any data.
