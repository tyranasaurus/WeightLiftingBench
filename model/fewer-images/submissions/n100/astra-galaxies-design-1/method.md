The network subtracts a local sky estimate and uses Gaussian apertures on concentric rings to measure isophotal widths, colour gradients, 180-degree asymmetry, and changes in position angle. A second branch measures angular harmonics in candidate disk planes and compares their phase changes with logarithmic spirals of either handedness. Other branches look for two resolved nuclei separated by a brightness saddle and compare the central light against individually normalized analytic models of ellipses, barred and unbarred spirals, one-sided tails, pairs, and thin disks with or without bulges. Hand-written ReLU rules combine the evidence into the ten class scores. The builder derives the filters and weights from formulas and fixed morphological assumptions; it does not read images, labels, or external parameter files.
