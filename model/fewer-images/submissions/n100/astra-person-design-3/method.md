The network scans an image pyramid for local evidence of people. One branch tests RGB skin-color inequalities, face-versus-surround color contrast, and the relative brightness of eyes, cheeks, nose and mouth. Other branches compare a head, shoulders, torso and legs to their flanks, match oriented edges to analytic silhouettes, or look for skin patches separated by plausible head-to-hand distances. Spatial maxima and weaker votes from other scales are combined with foreground isolation and scene layout to give one person score; the no-person score is zero.

The builder constructs the filters from formulas (ellipses, Gaussians, derivatives, line segments and color differences) with hand-specified anatomical proportions and decision rules. It reads no images or descriptors and uses no fitting. The fifth and final labeled check classified 120 of 200 supplied photos correctly (92/100 no-person and 28/100 person); this is not an independent test-set result.
