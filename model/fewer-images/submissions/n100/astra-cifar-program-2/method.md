I built each class from hand-specified 3D parts: for example, a car has a glasshouse and wheels, a deer has long jointed legs and upright ears, and a ship has a tapered hull. A fixed-seed renderer varies the viewpoint, proportions, materials, lighting, and background. Each render becomes its own template, encoded using pooled oriented edges, local brightness contrasts, colour, and contrast against the image margins; no class prototype or normalization statistic is computed across renders.

At prediction time the program compares a few translated and reflected views of a photo with individual templates. It combines the strongest matches with hand-written scene cues, such as rippled water below a horizon or a dark undercarriage over neutral pavement. Neither the geometry, descriptors, nor decision rules were fit to photographs or selected by scoring generated images.
