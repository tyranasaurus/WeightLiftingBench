The classifier subtracts the sky background, locates the central nucleus and traces equal-brightness contours at several levels. It uses a light bridge and two resolved peaks as evidence for a merger, and compares opposite angular sectors to detect tidal tails. Contour flattening and disk thickness distinguish smooth and edge-on systems; in deprojected disk coordinates, angular Fourier components and their change with radius distinguish a straight bar from winding arms. Color gradients provide supporting evidence rather than defining a class on their own.

The sampling grids, Gaussian filters, Fourier bases and decision rules are handwritten. I visually inspected raw contact sheets and used the permitted labeled checks to diagnose failures; no image, descriptor, class average or learned weight is stored. `build.py` writes the source directly, with no data or constants file. The final labeled check classified 562 of the 1,000 supplied images correctly; this is a development check, not held-out accuracy.
