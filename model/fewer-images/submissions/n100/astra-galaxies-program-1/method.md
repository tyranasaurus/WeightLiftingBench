The classifier estimates the sky and central light peak separately in each image. It measures connected equal-brightness contours and samples elliptical rings aligned to the galaxy. A hand-written decision tree looks for paired nuclei with a shared envelope, one-sided tidal light, and differences in disk thickness and colour between the nucleus and outskirts. On projected disks it compares angular Fourier phases against logarithmic spiral winding and looks for a centrally elongated component of older, redder stars as evidence of a bar.

I wrote the filters, measurements and rules directly, inspected labeled montages, and used the five permitted labeled checks to diagnose failure modes. There are no learned weights, example templates, or external constants. `build.py` emits `solution.py` from embedded source without reading any data. The final labeled check classified 477 of 1,000 supplied examples correctly; this is not a held-out result.
