I specified each letter in palm-width units with finger lengths, joint angles, thumb placement and a range of camera orientations. For side-facing signs the renderer also supports a hand-authored side elevation. The builder renders individual depth-buffered views with fixed-seed variation in articulation, proportions and lighting; it computes a separate normalized descriptor for each view and never reads a dataset or pools statistics across views.

At inference, the program compares oriented gradients at two resolutions, searches over translation and scale, and permits small local displacements of finger regions. Directional second derivatives also score narrow finger ridges and the valleys between adjacent fingers. The score also checks for edges along the projected hand boundary and for contrast inside the projected hand relative to the rest of the frame. A separate, confidence-weighted comparison describes the estimated foreground in rings and angular sectors around its centroid; this preserves information about finger spread that can disappear from a local edge match. The supplied images were used for qualitative inspection and the logged checks, not as stored examples or fitted parameters.
