# Method

**Architecture.** A hand-wired "measure, then apply rules" network (about 10.1 M weights, almost all zero), built only from conv / dense / relu / add / reshape / maxpool:

1. A 1x1 conv + relu turns the image into a soft mask (0 below 4% grey, 1 above 10% grey).
2. One dense layer takes running sums of the mask in the four directions; relu clips them to "is there item at or to the left / right / above / below this pixel".
3. A 1x1 conv + relu combines those four maps and the mask into 17 per-pixel logic maps: inside the row's extent, enclosed hole, gap open to the bottom (between trouser legs, under a hem or heel), gap open to the top (neckline), empty space under an overhang (under a short sleeve), empty top-left / top-right corner (above a shoe's toe, beside a bag handle), opening above a sole.
4. Four small side branches from formulas: a 3x3 erosion (interior pixels), a "dark valley" filter on the raw image (a pixel darker than the pixels two to its left and right, averaged over five rows — blurred gaps between legs or between sleeve and body), a centre-line filter (zip / button line), and a 3-tall test on the opening map.
5. Dense layers sum these maps over regions into a few dozen shape measurements (height, width, outline width in bands of rows, sleeve overhang, leg gap, hem gap, hole in the top part, left/right asymmetry, thin-sole columns, toe-end and heel-end height, ...).
6. A rule table: 21 prototypes over the 10 classes (e.g. T-shirt, tank top, trousers with gap, trousers closed, three dress shapes, six kinds of bag, low sandal, heeled sandal, boot with flat top, boot with tall back). Each prototype is a sum of capped piecewise-linear penalties "this measurement should lie in this range" built from relu hinges; scores are capped at 0 and the class score is the max over its prototypes (maxpool).

**How the weights were produced.** `build.py` reads no files. Filters and logic come from formulas; every threshold and range in the rule table is typed by hand from general knowledge of the items' proportions in a 28-pixel frame (a T-shirt body about half as wide as long with sleeves only over the top third; long sleeves keeping the outline wide to the hem; trousers with a leg gap; dresses with a narrow waist; shoes pointing left, sneakers low, boots with a full-height shaft, sandals thin and open; bags as symmetric boxes, often with a handle loop). Nothing was trained, fitted, averaged or copied from images. I looked at the 100 training images as pictures only; no pixel values, coordinates or statistics from them were computed or typed in.

**Checks.** Images I generated (rectangles, L-shapes, rings, bars) were used only as unit tests that each measurement equals its analytically known value and that the compiled rule table equals a direct evaluation of the rules; no number was chosen from them. The five allowed runs on the training images (logged in `checks.log`) gave 72, 71, 77, 77 and 76 correct out of 100. After each one I revised rules by reasoning about why a class failed (for example: a sneaker's shadow / sole strip is not a sandal strap; a boot's pull tab should not hide its shaft).

**Caveats.** The submitted build differs from the one scored in check 5 (76/100) by three rule-table lines, changed after that check and therefore not re-checked on real images: the shirt sleeve-gap term and the sandal flatness range were put back to the values used in checks 3–4, and the sneaker height limit was loosened. Known weak spots on the training images: shirts are rarely recognised (1–2 of 10), coats and pullovers are confused with each other, high-top sneakers are read as boots, and chunky sandals as sneakers or boots. I did not train the same architecture by gradient descent, so I cannot say how this compares with that reference; a hand-written rule table should be expected to fall short of it.
