The network uses fixed luminance and colour filters, then expands a seed from the image centre across connected bright pixels to suppress unrelated sky objects. Gaussian apertures and isophotal moments measure apparent axis ratio, extent, concentration, and bulge prominence; polar Fourier and log-spiral filters measure mirror asymmetry, bar-like elongation, and winding in either direction. A separate multiscale detector looks for a resolved second nucleus above a connecting saddle. Hand-written piecewise-linear rules combine these measurements into ten class scores. All filters and coefficients are generated by `build.py` from equations and morphology assumptions, without reading data or fitting weights.
