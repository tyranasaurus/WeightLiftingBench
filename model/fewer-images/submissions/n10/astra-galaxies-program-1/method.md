The classifier subtracts a per-channel sky estimate, locates the central galaxy, and measures the shape of its connected isophotes (contours of equal brightness). It tests for a second resolved nucleus and light bridging the two nuclei, and checks for tidal extensions by comparing opposite sectors of the galaxy. Axis ratio, vertical thickness, core concentration, and colour distinguish smooth galaxies from edge-on disks. For spirals, it samples the deprojected disk in log-polar coordinates: straight, opposed red ridges support a bar, while angular Fourier modes and curved blue-light ridges give evidence for arms and their pitch.

I wrote the filters and decision rules by hand from these morphological criteria. I visually inspected the example images and used four labeled sandbox checks to identify failure modes; no training images, measured training descriptors, fitted parameters, or template banks are stored in the program. `build.py` writes the complete standalone source without reading any data. Each image is classified independently with no randomness.
